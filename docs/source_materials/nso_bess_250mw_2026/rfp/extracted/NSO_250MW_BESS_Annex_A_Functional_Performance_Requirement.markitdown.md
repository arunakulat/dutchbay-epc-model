A.01. INTRODUCTION
This schedule specifies Technical Limits based on preliminary modelling and
assessment of the Facility and Network.
In connection with its award of the project, the project proponent will provide NSO
with its proposed Technical Limits together with supporting technical information
provided by the Construction Developer and equipment supplier(s) as required by
the project proponent in accordance with the Specifications (including power
systems model in PSS®E and PSCAD format). Any deviations to the Technical Limits
herein shall be identified, and demonstration provided that these do not adversely
impact the stability, reliability and power quality of the Network.
NSO may request additional data to determine the appropriateness of the Technical
Limits.
NSO’s feedback on the acceptability of the Company’s proposed Technical Limits
shall not be unreasonably withheld or delayed and shall be deemed to be given
unless NSO informs the Company in writing of any disagreement (giving reasons)
within sixty (60) days of receipt by it of the information provided by the Company
pursuant to this Schedule.
During the Supply Period, configurable settings for Technical Limits must be
reconfigured at the request of NSO to maintain stability following changes in the
network. Any such changes shall be agreed with NSO.

(b) Grid integration and control performance complied with the specification as

rated current for 10 seconds.

module owner shall not unreasonably withhold consent.

A.05.03. Frequency support capability
(a) The BESS must be capable of responding independently on a configurable
droop curve in response to frequency deviations, without receiving external
control signals from NSO.
(b) Droop set point shall be set based on studies conducted by the NSO as part
of the design of the Facility for the purpose of maintaining network stability.
Droop setting shall be able to set between 1% to 9% as per the NSO
discretion. If NSO did not disclose any Droop value, then it shall be set at 4%.
(c) During a sudden frequency drop BESS shall mimic an inertia response of a
synchronous machine through its grid forming control system. This synthetic
inertia response shall be configured by the NSO as part of the design of the
Facility for the purpose of maintaining network stability.
(d) The maximum inertia time constant of the grid-forming electrochemical energy
storage system shall be no less than 20 seconds, with an inertia response
activation time ≤5 ms. The system shall allow flexible adjustment based on
grid requirements.
(e) The response time for primary frequency regulation shall be <0.2 s, with
active power adjustment deviation ≤2%.

A.05.09. Response to disturbances following contingency events
(a) BESS must remain in continuous uninterrupted operation for any disturbance
caused by:
i. a credible contingency event
ii. a three-phase fault in a transmission system cleared by all relevant
primary protection systems (250 millisecond)
iii. a two phase to ground, phase to phase or phase to ground fault in a
transmission system cleared in:

a. the longest time expected to be taken for a relevant breaker fail
protection system to clear the fault; or
b. if a protection system referred to in subparagraph (a) is not
installed, the greater of the time specified by NSO (or if none is
specified, 550 milliseconds) and the longest time expected to be
taken for all relevant primary protection systems to clear the fault; or
iv. a three phase, two phase to ground, phase to phase or phase to
ground fault in a distribution network cleared in:
a. the longest time expected to be taken for the breaker fail protection
system to clear the fault; or
b. if a protection system specified by NSO, the greater of 1000
milliseconds and the longest time expected to be taken for all
relevant primary protection systems to clear the fault,
provided that the event is not one that would disconnect the production unit
from the power system by removing network elements from service.
(b) BESS and each of its production units must remain in continuous
uninterrupted operation for a series of up to 6 disturbances within any five-
minute period caused by any combination of the events described in
paragraph (c) where:
i. up to four of the disturbances (two LLLG faults, one LL and one LG
fault) cause the voltage at the connection point to drop below 50% of
normal voltage
ii. up to one disturbance is cleared by a breaker fail protection system or
similar back-up protection system
iii. up to one disturbance causes the voltage at the connection point to
vary within the ranges under clause A.05.08. .
iv. provided that none of the events would result in:
a. the islanding of the generating system or integrated resource
system or cause a material reduction in power transfer capability
by removing network elements from service
b. the cumulative time that voltage at the connection point is lower
than 90% of normal voltage exceeding 1,800 milliseconds within
any five-minute period
c. the time integral, within any five-minute period, of the difference
between 90% of normal voltage and the voltage at the
connection point when the voltage at the connection point is
lower than 90% of normal voltage exceeding 1 pu second.
(c) BESS shall provide Inertia support during a fault

A.05.10. Reactive current injection and active power recovery during the fault
(a) Subject to any changed power system conditions or energy source availability
beyond the BESS reasonable control, BESS respect of the types of faults

supplying to or absorbing reactive current from the network:
i. to assist the maintenance of power system voltages during the fault:
a. BESS inverter shall inject reactive current during the fault (within
their capability) based on the amount of remaining system
voltage. Amount of reactive current during the contingency shall
be equal to 2% for every 1% drop in voltage at the POC as a
default value. Further, the BESS unit shall be capable of
providing reactive current of x% per 1% drop in voltage at POC
where x% shall be set between 1% to 6%.
b. Additional reactive current injection during fault shall be in
addition to the steady state reactive current given at that time by
the BESS.
c. during the disturbance and maintained until connection point
voltage recovers to between 90% and 110% of normal voltage,
or such other range agreed with the NSO; and
ii. from 100 milliseconds after clearance of the fault, active power shall
recover to at least 95% of the level existing just prior to the fault.
(b) NSO may request the contribution of positive, negative and zero sequence

specified within the range (0.9 pu-1.1 pu), either continuously or in
steps.
ii. It shall be possible to operate voltage control with or without a dead
band around the setpoint. The dead band shall be adjustable and
agreed with NSO.
iii. Voltage droop control mode shall include the capability to change
reactive power output based on a combination of a modified voltage
set-point and an additional instructed reactive power component (i.e.
voltage-reactive power droop). The slope (i.e. voltage droop) shall be
adjustable in the range (typically 2% -12%).
(c) The BESS shall be capable of operating at a designated reactive power value
specified by the NSO as long as the same is within the reactive power
capability of the BESS.

(d) The BESS shall be capable of controlling the power factor to a target at the
POC, while respecting Reactive Power Capability and Reactive Power

A.05.15. Set point response
(a) The BESS inverter must be able to respond independently to separate
command set points from National System Control Centre (NSCC) for the
following performance characteristics under normal operating conditions:
i. MW
ii. Mvar
iii. power factor
iv. voltage
(b) Set point response shall be by bias of the relevant droop curve and grid
support functionality shall be maintained, within the active and reactive power
capability limits of the BESS.
(c) The behavior of the relevant performance characteristic following receipt of a
set point command at the Facility SCADA, subject to any pre-set ramp rate
limitations, and limitations on the active and reactive power capability limits of
the BESS, shall be as follows:
i. Set point response must commence within 50 ms.
ii. Commanded set point value must be achieved within 300 ms
iii. Overshoot of the commanded set point value shall not exceed 10% of
the value of the change in set point value.
iv. Settling time, to within 1% of full-scale output for the relevant
performance characteristic, must be achieved within 400 ms.
v. BESS shall automatically notify NSCC when its state of charge moves
outside the normal operating state of charge limits as described in
Functional Specifications and reduce the active power set point to zero
in a manner pre-agreed with NSO.
A.05.16. Priority to Active or Reactive power contribution
(a) The BESS shall be capable of operating in the active or reactive power priority
modes, as requested by NSO, while meeting continuous operating rating and
short-term overload ratings. This includes utilizing the full rating (continuous
and short-term overload) of the inverter current capability and operating in
zero active power transfer. Taking into account the capabilities of the BESS,
NSO may inform on case by case basis whether active power contribution or
reactive power contribution has the priority during low or high voltage
operation.
(b) If not specified active power control shall have the priority.

A.05.17. Control interaction with other generating devices
(a) Control Interaction is a specific issue that can impact the coordinated
operation of Power Electronic devices in a local area. Control systems of
dynamic devices can interact in an undesirable manner resulting in unstable or
poorly damped oscillations following system disturbances such as fault
recovery.
(b) The BESS shall design the equipment to avoid such undesirable interactions,
and the acceptable operation demonstrated through interconnection studies.
(c) The BESS shall be capable of contributing to the damping of power
oscillations in the connected AC Transmission system. The Developer shall
require to provide all required data control system models including
parameters for of any small signal modulation controls such as power
oscillation damping (POD) controls, or sub-synchronous oscillation damping
controls.
(d) The grid-forming BESS shall have autonomous damping suppression
functionality. When low-frequency oscillations (0.2 Hz–2.5 Hz) occur, the
system shall suppress oscillations, with active power variation limited to 10%–
30% Pn.
(e) The BESS shall be capable to inject or absorb active phase jump power into
the system immediately due to the change of phase angle in the event of
disturbance or failure of the system
(f) Regarding the sub-synchronous torsional interaction (SSTI) damping control,
the BESS shall be capable of contributing to electrical damping at torsional
oscillation frequencies. The SSTI studies shall be undertaken by the NSO.
The studies shall identify the conditions, if any, where SSTI exists and
propose any necessary mitigation measures.
(g) The BESS shall be capable of finding stable operation points with a minimum
change in active power flow and voltage level, during and after any planned or
unplanned change in the BESS or AC Transmission System to which it is
connected.
(h) The grid-forming BESS shall receive and respond to AGC (Automatic
Generation Control) commands from the grid dispatch, with a regulation range
of -100% to +100% Pn and steady-state active power deviation ≤2% Pn. It
shall also receive and respond to AVC (Automatic Voltage Control)
commands, with steady-state reactive power deviation ≤2% Pn.
(i) The BESS shall support both grid-following and grid-forming control modes,
enabling online switching between the two.
21

(j) The grid-following mode shall operate stably under a minimum short-circuit
ratio (SCR) of 1.2, while the grid-forming mode shall operate stably under a
minimum SCR of 1.0.
A.05.18. Fault current
(a) the contribution of the BESS to the fault current on the connecting network
through its connection point must not exceed the contribution level that will
ensure that the total fault current can be safely interrupted by the circuit
breakers of the connecting network and safely carried by the connecting
network for the duration of the applicable breaker fail protection system fault
clearance times, as specified for the relevant connection point by the NSO
(b) BESS connected plant must be capable of withstanding fault current through
the connection point up to the higher of:
i. the level specified by NSO as per the clause A.04.
ii. the highest level of current at the connection point that can be safely
interrupted by the circuit breakers of the connecting network and safely
carried by the connecting network for the duration of the applicable
breaker fail protection system fault clearance times, as specified by the
NSO
iii. circuit breaker provided to isolate a generating unit, generating system,
bidirectional unit or integrated resource system from the network must
be capable of breaking, without damage or restrike, the maximum fault
currents that could reasonably be expected to flow through the circuit
breaker for any fault in the network or in the generating unit, generating
system, bidirectional unit or integrated resource system, as specified in
the connection agreement.
A.05.19. Loss of communications
(a) In the event of loss of communications, the set point response of the BESS
shall automatically revert to a state as agreed with NSO during design of the
Facility.
(b) Upon restoration of communications, the BESS plant shall not automatically
restore operations but shall assess and issue ready status. Restart of
operations shall only be commenced following instruction from NSO.
A.05.20. Safe shutdown
(a) In the event network fault that leads to power system conditions outside the
requirements of this specification, the BESS must automatically execute a
safe and controlled shutdown.
(b) Upon restoration of standard operating conditions, the BESS plant shall not
automatically restore operations but shall assess and issue ready status.
Restart of operations shall only be commenced following instruction from
NSO.
22

(c) Subject to the limits of the backup power supply of the Facility,
communications and essential functions shall remain operational throughout a
safe shutdown event.
A.05.21. Protection from power system disturbances
(a) BESS to be automatically disconnected from the power system in response to
abnormal conditions arising from the power system, the relevant protection
system or control system must not disconnect the BESS for:
i. conditions for which it must remain in continuous uninterrupted
operation or
ii. conditions it must withstand under the Grid Connection Code; and
iii. a relevant system (as defined in paragraph (a)i connected to a
transmission system must have facilities to automatically and rapidly
reduce its generation:
(b) NSO may require the BESS to be automatically disconnected by a local or
remote-control scheme whenever the part of the network to which it is
connected has been disconnected from the national grid, forming an island
that supplies load.
(c) Notwithstanding clauses in Grid Connection Code or in this schedule,
BESS may be automatically disconnected from the power system under any
of the following conditions:
a. in accordance with an ancillary services agreement between the
BESS and NSO
b. where a source of load that is not part of the BESS has the same
connection point as the generating system and NSO agree that the
disconnection would in effect be under-frequency load shedding
c. where the generating system or integrated resource system is
automatically disconnected under, Protection systems that impact on
power system security or by an emergency frequency control scheme
d. where the generating system or integrated resource system is
automatically disconnected under Protection to trip plant for unstable
operation; or
e. in accordance with an agreement between the Developer and NSO
(including an agreement in relation to an emergency control scheme) to
provide a service that NSO agrees is necessary to maintain or restore
power system security in the event of a specified contingency event.
